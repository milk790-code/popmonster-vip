Angel Coating Guard Full Application Guide:
The Science and Steps of 1:1000 Dilution

This guide breaks down how POP MONSTER's Angel Coating Guard works: the application principle, dilution ratio, steps, and common mistakes. Whether you're new to detailing or a pro, you'll find where it fits your routine.

What is a paint maintenance spray?

Traditional paint care products fall roughly into three tiers: wax, sealant, and coating. Wax is the oldest option — it gives short-term shine and light water beading, but it doesn't last long. Sealants add polymers and can hold up for 1–3 months. Coatings move into inorganic silane or graphene chemistry, protecting anywhere from six months to two years.

Angel Coating Guard is a paint maintenance spray you use right after washing your car. It's a purpose-made ester-based formula that forms a single, stable layer — there's no layering reaction. It soaks into the paint, color-change wrap, and PPF's film layer, so if things aren't properly cleaned beforehand, you'll run into problems.

Here's the key feature: right after washing, while the car is still wet, spray the diluted mix over the whole car, go over it gently once with a clean wash mitt, then rinse and dry it off — and you're done. If you don't care about "maximum protection" but want your car to look freshly waxed after every wash, this is a great value pick.

The core numbers behind Angel Coating Guard

POP MONSTER's Angel Coating Guard is built around three numbers:

  • 1ml = 1L: the 1:1000 ratio is for a manual pump sprayer — 1ml of concentrate to 1000ml of purified water. PA foam lances use a different ratio, so follow the bottle label, or tell us on WhatsApp which sprayer you're using and we'll give you the right ratio.
  • 3 hours in sunlight: after 3 hours of sun exposure, surface gloss and feel hit their peak.
  • 30-day conservative protection: holds up through 3–4 normal washes.
Design philosophy

These three numbers aren't there to impress — they tell you what this product is designed for. It's not a long-lasting product you apply once a year; it's a maintenance product you top up once a month when you wash your car.

Four steps to apply it (anyone can do this)

01
Dilute it first
Get a clean manual pump sprayer (a 1-liter chemical-grade PE sprayer works well). Add 1ml of concentrate (use a 5ml dropper for accuracy) to 1000ml of purified water (tap water isn't recommended). Cap it and shake gently to mix — no need to shake hard. 1ml to 1000ml is the ratio for a manual pump sprayer; PA foam lances use a different ratio, so follow the bottle label, and WhatsApp us first if you're not sure.
02
Spray it on right after washing
Wash the car clean first, including removing oil film and degreasing. Once you're done with a normal wash, don't dry it off — keep the surface wet. Spray it evenly over the whole car, and don't miss any spots.
03
Go over it with a wash mitt
Using a clean wash mitt, gently go over the whole surface from top to bottom. Use light pressure — rubbing the same spot over and over can leave marks. One pass is enough; this isn't scrubbing or buffing.
04
Rinse it off, then dry
Rinse once with clean water starting from the roof to wash away the excess coating. Then dry it off as usual with a chamois or a water-absorbing towel. At this point the water beads tighter and dries off cleaner than it would without the coating.

Common mistakes and answers

Mistake #1: Is more concentrated better?

No. Don't make it stronger or weaker on your own — follow the ratio on the bottle. The 1:1000 ratio (1cc of concentrate to 1000ml of purified water) is for a manual pump sprayer. PA foam lances use a different ratio, so don't copy someone else's mix. WhatsApp us first if you're not sure.

Mistake #2: Can it replace a coating?

Not entirely. Angel Coating Guard is a maintenance product — spray it over the whole car right after washing, while it's still wet. It works on paint, PPF, color-change wrap, glass, and plastic trim. Conservative protection lasts up to a month; it's a top-up you do while washing, not a one-time coating meant to last a long time.

Mistake #3: Will it damage the paint?

No. Angel Coating Guard forms a single, stable layer, so there's no layering reaction. Repeated applications give the same result. Before applying, check the surface material and make sure it's clean. To remove it, mist on our heavy-duty leather cleaner concentrate, or pre-wash with an anionic surfactant — it comes off after several washes.

Mistake #4: Can I use it on a wrapped car?

Yes. Angel Coating Guard's purpose-made ester-based formula works well on PPF and color-change wraps, improving dirt resistance and how long the coating lasts. It's suitable for TPU, PVC, and other film types.

When not to use it

  • Paint with heavy oxidation or swirl marks: polish first, then apply the coating. You need to look at the oxidation before deciding — if it hasn't eaten through the clear coat, polishing can bring it back. If it's soaked all the way through and still won't come back after polishing, that section of paint is beyond saving. That's the paint, not the compound.
  • Fresh paint straight out of the paint booth: WhatsApp us first to ask whether you can apply it and how long to wait.
  • Surface with heavy acid-rain marks or water spots: treat with a water spot remover first.
  • Surface with a heavy wax residue buildup: strip it with a degreaser first.

Wrap-up

Angel Coating Guard is built around one idea: anyone should be able to get pro-level results. It's not meant to replace a coating — it's meant to solve the most everyday, most overlooked problem: how to keep that clean feeling after you wash your car.

Check how tight the water beads, how cleanly it dries off, and how clean it feels — those three things tell you right away if it's right for you.
