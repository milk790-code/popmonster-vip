Polishing Pad Selection Guide:
Wool, Cutting, Polishing, and Finishing Pads

The polishing pad is the compound's partner. Pick the wrong pad and you've wasted your time. This guide covers material, density, and hardness so you can choose the right pad.

Why the polishing pad matters

A lot of people think the compound is what matters most, but the compound supplies the cutting abrasive and the pad determines how that cutting power gets applied. The same compound will give different results with different pads. The pad itself doesn't set the grit - the grit comes from the compound - so you need to think about pad and compound together.

On a pro shop's tool cart, the polishing pads alone come in 5 to 8 types, each one matched to a specific situation.

The four main types of polishing pads

Wool pad

Made from real or synthetic wool fiber. This is the highest-cutting pad type, and it also builds up heat the fastest. Good for heavy cutting with coarse compounds like 80 grit. The POP MONSTER RO Custom Wool Pad is a synthetic wool type paired with a high-resilience foam backing to lower the risk of burning the paint during heavy cutting. It doesn't shed fiber and heats up slowly, with a high-resilience foam backing plate.

Cutting foam pad

Usually orange or maroon, with high density and a large open-cell structure. Used with 600-grit medium-cut compound, this is the pad you'll reach for most in everyday polishing. Medium cutting power, medium heat buildup, easy to control. If you're new and only want to buy one pad, the cutting foam pad is the safest place to start.

Polishing foam pad

Usually white or light-colored, denser than the cutting foam pad. Gentle cutting power, mainly for medium-level correction and boosting gloss. Used with 800 to 1000-grit compound.

Finishing foam pad

Usually black or gray, the highest density and the finest surface. Used with fine 1000 to 1500-grit compound for the final mirror finish. The POP MONSTER RO Custom Mirror Foam Pad (Black) is a high-density foam pad made for RO machines, with GA/DA custom versions also available. It comes after the cutting and polishing foam pads as the last step, for finishing and brightening only - it's not a heavy-cut pad. For deeper marks, use the cutting foam pad or the wool pad instead.

How density and hardness relate

How do you tell if the density is good?

  • Pinch the pad between your fingers - if it springs back quickly, that means high density and good quality
  • Press it with your hand - if the surface stays flat and doesn't dent, that means the structure is stable
  • Check the surface pores - even in size and evenly spaced means the manufacturing is consistent

Two typical problems with low-quality pads: uneven pressure distribution (causes wave marks in the paint); and poor durability (deforms or comes unglued after 5 to 10 uses).

Why the backing plate matters

A backing plate that's too hard transfers vibration straight to the paint and can damage it. One that's too soft absorbs the cutting power and lowers efficiency. POP MONSTER pads are custom-made for RO, GA, and DA machines, with a choice of backing and style: the RO Custom Wool Pad uses a high-resilience foam backing, while the RO Custom Mirror Foam Pad uses a heat-stable EVA backing plus a specially made cushioning layer. There are also versions built specifically for GA/DA vibration frequencies.

Choosing a pad size

  • 5 inch is the standard size: good for a typical home-use DA polisher
  • 6 inch is for large panels: hoods, roofs, and other large flat areas
  • 3 inch is for detail work: bumper grooves, mirrors, and corners

For beginners, we suggest a 5 inch pad as your main one and a 3 inch as backup - that covers about 90% of situations.

Cleaning and care

We haven't published official cleaning and care instructions for our pads yet. Below is the general approach for polishing pads - if you're not sure, ask on WhatsApp first.

  1. Rinse it right away after every use - don't let the compound dry on the pad
  2. Clean with a pH-neutral cleaner and a soft brush
  3. Air-dry it in the shade, not direct sun - sun exposure makes the foam brittle
  4. Store it somewhere dry and ventilated to prevent mold

Suggested starter kit: 1 wool pad (for 80 grit) + 2 cutting foam pads (for 600 grit) + 1 finishing pad (for 1000 grit) - that covers you from heavy cutting through to the mirror finish. The wool pad cuts hard, so if you're new to this, practice with the medium-correction and finishing pads first before moving up to the heavy-cut pad. This is a starting point, not the only way to go - adjust the pad and grit combination based on how the job looks as you work.
